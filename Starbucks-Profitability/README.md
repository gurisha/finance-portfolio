# Starbucks Profitability & Operational Analytics

# OBJECTIVE

To evaluate product- and store-level profitability across a multi-store retail operation, with a focus on identifying profit concentration, margin efficiency differences, and structural underperformance drivers. The goal was to simulate how finance and operations teams assess economic contribution beyond top-line revenue.

# DATA USED

* Simulated multi-store retail dataset
• Product-level revenue and unit sales data
• Unit cost components (ingredient, packaging, labor)
• Store-level performance data across a six-month period
• Portfolio-level revenue and gross profit totaling approximately $1.7M and $827K respectively

# ANALYSIS PERFORMED

• Modeled unit-level cost structure to calculate revenue, gross profit, and gross margin
• Built product-level profit contribution analysis
• Conducted store-level gross margin benchmarking against portfolio average
• Identified margin variance drivers independent of sales volume
• Developed executive dashboard separating analytical and visualization layers

# KEY FINDINGS

• Profit is highly concentrated within two core products, exposing the portfolio to contribution risk
• Revenue does not directly translate to economic contribution due to margin variability
• Store performance variance is driven primarily by margin efficiency rather than demand differences
• Only one store operates above the portfolio gross margin benchmark

# BUSINESS IMPLICATION

• Margin protection on high-contribution products is critical to maintaining portfolio profitability
• Underperforming stores require cost structure and pricing review before pursuing volume growth
• Portfolio-level margin benchmarking provides a scalable framework for ongoing operational performance monitoring
